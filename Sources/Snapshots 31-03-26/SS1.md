# Historical Precedents: Middle East Conflicts & Financial Markets

## 1973 Oil Crisis

- Oil prices quadrupled from $3 to nearly $12 per barrel[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis)
    
- Severe drain on US gold reserves, contributing to collapse of Bretton Woods system[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis)
    
- Caused both inflationary and deflationary macroeconomic impacts[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis)
    

## 2003 Iraq War

- US 10-year Treasury yield fell from 3.95% to 3.55% (40bp drop) as investors sought safety
    
- Brent crude averaged low-to-mid $30s per barrel pre-invasion[](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/bond-market-revisits-familiar-wartime-playbook-with-very-different-oil-backdrop/)
    
- After hostilities began, 10-year yield popped back to 4.1% area[](https://think.ing.com/articles/rates-what-can-we-glean-from-the-iraq-war/)
    

## 1991 Gulf War

- Initial stock market surge: Dow Jones up 4.6% (114.6 points) on hopes of brief conflict[](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-01-18-fi-122-story.html)
    
- Dollar fell sharply against major currencies (nearly 4 pfennigs vs German mark, 2 cents vs pound, 4+ yen vs yen)[](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-01-18-fi-122-story.html)
    
- Gold prices plunged $30.10/ounce on initial success reports[](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-01-18-fi-122-story.html)
    
- Federal funds rate fell from 6.688% to 6.25%[](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-01-18-fi-122-story.html)
    

## Current Iran Tensions (Early 2026)

- 10-year Treasury yield dropped below 4% to just above 3.9% then rebounded above 4% and touched 4.1% (only 20bp swing vs 40bp in 2003)[](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/bond-market-revisits-familiar-wartime-playbook-with-very-different-oil-backdrop/)
    
- Today's much higher oil price baseline ($80-90 range vs $30s in 2003) raises inflation stakes[](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/bond-market-revisits-familiar-wartime-playbook-with-very-different-oil-backdrop/)
    
- Short-end inflation pressure showing through elevated 2-year breakevens[](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/bond-market-revisits-familiar-wartime-playbook-with-very-different-oil-backdrop/)
    

# Analysis: Potential Impacts of Escalation with US Ground Forces

## Currency Movements

- **Immediate impact**: Dollar likely to strengthen further as safe-haven demand intensifies
    
- **Historical pattern**: In 1991, dollar fell sharply on short-war expectations; with potential for prolonged conflict, safe-haven flows may dominate[](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-01-18-fi-122-story.html)
    
- **Longer-term**: If conflict triggers sustained inflation, dollar strength may be tested as Fed faces policy dilemmas
    

## Bond Prices & Yields

- **Initial reaction**: Flight-to-quality would push Treasury prices up (yields down), particularly at longer end
    
- **Inflation risk**: Higher oil prices could reverse this trend if inflation expectations rise significantly[](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/bond-market-revisits-familiar-wartime-playbook-with-very-different-oil-backdrop/)
    
- **Yield curve**: Potential for further steepening if short-end rises on inflation fears while long-end benefits from safe-haven flows[](https://www.cmegroup.com/videos/2026/03/27/2-year-treasury-note-futures-bounced-off-lows-as-curve-steepened.html)
    
- **Magnitude**: Given today's higher oil price base, inflationary impacts could be more pronounced than in 2003[](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/bond-market-revisits-familiar-wartime-playbook-with-very-different-oil-backdrop/)
    

## Commodity Markets

- **Oil prices**: Likely to spike significantly above current $96+/barrel levels, potentially reaching triple digits if supply disruptions occur
    
- **Natural gas**: European prices already above €50/MWh could see further increases
    
- **Gold**: Historically strong performer during geopolitical crises; current rebound trend could accelerate[](https://www.caixabankresearch.com/en/publications/financial-markets-daily-report/05-march-2026)
    
- **Industrial metals**: Mixed impact - demand concerns vs. inflation hedge properties
    

## Equity Markets

- **Short-term**: Initial volatility spike, potential for Asian markets to weaken further while US/Europe show mixed reactions[](https://www.caixabankresearch.com/en/publications/financial-markets-daily-report/05-march-2026)
    
- **Sector effects**: Defense stocks likely to benefit, consumer discretionary and industrials may face headwinds[](https://am.pictet.com/us/en/investment-views/multi-asset/2026/march-barometer-of-financial-markets-outlook)
    
- **Historical context**: 1991 showed initial rally on short-war hopes; prolonged conflict typically weighs on equities[](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-01-18-fi-122-story.html)
    

## Inflation & Monetary Policy

- **Immediate concern**: Oil price pass-through to headline inflation would be more immediate today given higher baseline prices[](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/bond-market-revisits-familiar-wartime-playbook-with-very-different-oil-backdrop/)
    
- **Policy dilemma**: Fed may face stagflationary pressures - rising inflation alongside potential growth slowdown
    
- **Breakeven inflation**: 2-year breakevens already elevated; further oil spikes could push these higher[](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/bond-market-revisits-familiar-wartime-playbook-with-very-different-oil-backdrop/)
    

## Key Differentiators from Past Conflicts

1. **Higher oil price base**: Today's $80-90/bbl vs $30s in 2003 means same percentage shock delivers larger absolute price increase[](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/bond-market-revisits-familiar-wartime-playbook-with-very-different-oil-backdrop/)
    
2. **Inflation environment**: Starting from 2.4% YoY inflation vs lower baselines in previous conflicts[](https://www.caixabankresearch.com/en/publications/financial-markets-daily-report/05-march-2026)
    
3. **Policy backdrop**: Central banks less accommodative than in 2003 era[](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/bond-market-revisits-familiar-wartime-playbook-with-very-different-oil-backdrop/)
    
4. **Geographic scope**: Potential Strait of Hormuz closure would affect ~20% of global oil supply[](https://www.caixabankresearch.com/en/publications/financial-markets-daily-report/12-march-2026)
    

The current environment suggests any escalation would likely trigger initial safe-haven flows (dollar strength, bond rally, gold gains) but with greater inflationary risks due to the higher oil price baseline, potentially leading to a more complex market reaction as the conflict evolves.
