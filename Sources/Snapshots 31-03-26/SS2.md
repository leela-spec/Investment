### **Historical Playbook: US Ground Force Escalation**

Your prediction of an escalation to ground forces changes the market "math" from a liquidity shock to a fiscal and structural shock. Historically, when the U.S. moves from "stand-off" strikes to "boots on the ground," three major patterns emerge:

#### **1. Currency: The "Safe-Haven Paradox"**

During both the **1991 Gulf War** and the **2003 Iraq War**, the USD initially surged on a flight-to-safety. However, once ground operations were underway, the narrative shifted toward the massive **fiscal deficit** required to fund the war.

- **The 1991 Experience:** The USD saw a "relief rally" once the ground war started, as the quick success removed the "uncertainty discount."
    
- **The 2003 Experience:** The USD actually entered a multi-year bear market starting in 2003. Investors began to worry about the "twin deficits" (fiscal and current account) exacerbated by a long-term occupation.
    
- **Predictive Shift:** In 2026, expect the USD to remain strong _until_ the ground war is confirmed, at which point a "sell the news" event could weaken the Dollar against the Euro or Gold as the cost of the war is tallied.
    

#### **2. Bonds: The Yield Curve Flattening**

Ground wars are expensive and inflationary. In 2003, the 10-year Treasury yield dropped to **3.55%** in the lead-up (fear) but popped back to **4.1%** the moment the invasion began.

- **Inflation Breakevens:** Short-term yields (2-year) typically spike because oil prices drive immediate CPI.
    
- **Long-Term Yields:** These are more sensitive to the "growth drain." If the market fears a ground war will lead to a multi-year quagmire, the yield curve will **invert or flatten** aggressively.
    
- **The 2026 Nuance:** Unlike 2003, we are already starting with a high debt-to-GDP ratio. A ground war now could force a "fiscal dominance" scenario where the Fed is pressured to keep yields low to help the government fund the war, even if inflation stays high.
    

#### **3. Commodities: "Buy the Rumor, Sell the Fact"**

Counter-intuitively, the _actual_ start of a ground war has often marked the **peak** for oil prices.

- **1991:** Oil spiked to $40/bbl before the war but crashed toward $20/bbl the week ground operations began.
    
- **2003:** Oil prices fell nearly **16%** the day before the 2003 invasion, as the "market" realized the supply disruption was already fully priced in.
    
- **Risk for 2026:** Because the Strait of Hormuz is involved today (unlike 1991 or 2003), a ground war might not trigger the traditional "sell-off" in oil unless the U.S. can quickly secure the shipping lanes.
