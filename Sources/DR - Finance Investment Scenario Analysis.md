## Scenarios

Framework note: Each scenario is written as (a) **structural drivers** (slow-moving), (b) **catalysts** (event accelerants), and (c) **plumbing/positioning** (how flows/hedging/leverage amplify moves). The “Mechanism narrative” and “Most likely sequencing” embed (b) and (c) explicitly.

### Scenario A: AI productivity boom / disinflationary growth

Mechanism narrative (cause → transmission → market effect):  
Cause → AI diffusion raises output per worker; capex/compute scales while labor stays resilient. citeturn0search0  
Transmission → unit labor costs fall; margins expand; disinflation broadens, easing the policy constraint.  
Market effect → risk assets grind higher; “long duration” works as real yields drift lower; volatility is structurally sold.

Market pattern expectations:
- Equities: ↑ (breadth improves; “AI users” catch up to “AI builders”)  
- Duration: ↑ (long duration outperforms short)  
- Credit: ↑ (spreads tighten; defaults subdued)  
- USD: ↔/↓ (mild weakening if global growth broadens)  
- Commodities/gold: ↔/↑ (gold supported as policy uncertainty hedge; cyclicals mixed)  
- Volatility: ↓ (spot and term structure normalize into contango)  
- Crypto: ↑ (liquidity beta dominates; narrative risk stays)

Dominant driver buckets: Technology/Regulation; Growth/Labor; Fiscal/Term premium

What would change my mind:
- AI capex collapses while productivity gains fail to appear in broad data  
- Sticky services inflation re-accelerates despite productivity narratives  
- Credit silently deteriorates (defaults up, lending standards tighter) while equities ignore it

Most likely sequencing: equities and AI capex optimism lead first; long bonds then reassert hedging power; late-cycle risk is “crowded quiet” (vol too cheap).

### Scenario B: AI displacement → demand shock → deflationary recession + credit stress

Market pattern expectations:

- Duration: ↑ (classic crisis bid; long duration outperforms)

- Credit stress remains isolated and is absorbed without broader tightening

Most likely sequencing: margins expand → layoffs rise → consumption cracks with a lag → credit loss recognition → policy response (cuts/liquidity) → only then does “cheap duration” fully hedge.

- Tariff threats reverse credibly (implementation fades, retaliation contained)

## Playbook

### Monthly decision system

Scoring method: score each signpost **0–2** monthly. Then compute scenario scores using either the direct score (**S**) or the inverted score (**2−S**) depending on the scenario’s directional signature.

Define the 7 signposts (S1–S7):
- S1 **AI labor balance**: 0 = broad white‑collar displacement accelerating; 1 = mixed; 2 = productivity up with labor resilient. citeturn0search0  
- S2 **Inflation stickiness**: 0 = disinflation broadening; 1 = mixed; 2 = sticky core / expectations firming. citeturn1search5  
- S3 **Tariffs/fragmentation impulse**: 0 = de‑escalation; 1 = noise; 2 = new implemented tariffs/retaliation. citeturn1search3  
- S4 **Term-premium / fiscal stress**: 0 = term premium stable; 1 = wobble; 2 = persistent steepening/auction weakness. citeturn2search2turn2search17  
- S5 **Credit stress / loss recognition**: 0 = benign; 1 = contained; 2 = spreads/defaults/marks worsening (incl. private credit headlines). citeturn2search3  
- S6 **USD weakness regime signal**: 0 = USD strengthening; 1 = range; 2 = broad USD downtrend persists. citeturn6search6  
- S7 **Capital rotation / reserve diversification**: 0 = steady U.S. bid; 1 = mixed; 2 = sustained evidence of diversification away from USD/U.S. assets. citeturn6search0turn6search11  

Scenario scores (simple formulas; max ~10):
- Scenario A (Good AI) = **S1 + (2−S2) + (2−S4) + (2−S5) + (2−S3)**  
- Scenario B (Bad AI) = **(2−S1) + (2−S2) + S5 + (2−S6) + S4**  
- Scenario C (Stagflation) = **S2 + S3 + S4 + (2−S1) + (2−S5)**  
- Scenario D (USD‑weak rotation) = **S6 + S7 + (2−S5) + (2−S4) + S1**  
- Scenario E (Debt-cycle shock) = **S4 + S5 + S2 + (2−S1) + (2−S6)**  

Trigger logic to activate/deactivate overlays:
- Compute scores monthly.
- **Primary overlay = highest score**, **secondary overlay = second highest** if within 1–2 points.
- Activate an overlay when its scenario score is **≥7** **and** rising vs prior month; reduce when **≤5** or when another scenario overtakes by **≥2 points**.
- Rebalancing guidance: **on trigger flips; otherwise periodic monthly check** (avoid over-trading).

### Crisis overlay

Purpose: protect against deflationary crash, forced deleveraging, and credit events (Scenario B, sometimes E).

Components (categories only):
- Cash/bills; short‑duration high grade  
- High‑grade sovereigns, **intermediate-to-long duration**  
- High‑quality “safe-haven” sovereigns **with explicit FX lens** (case analysis: CHF/SGD/NOK)  
- Quality/min‑vol equity style  
- Gold (monetary metal)  
- VIX‑futures‑linked volatility products (small, timed)  
- IG credit (selective; avoid duration‑heavy credit)  

When to activate:
- S5 (credit stress) prints 2 and rising  
- S1 collapses (2−S1 rises) alongside weakening consumption indicators  
- Funding stress / USD squeeze signs (S6 = 0 and cross‑currency basis stress) citeturn6search14  
- Equity breadth breaks and vol term structure starts flattening/inverting (contango → backwardation)

When to reduce/exit:
- Credit spreads peak and start tightening; S5 falls back to 0–1  
- USD funding stress eases and volatility mean-reverts

Key failure mode:
- Crisis morphs into stagflation/term-premium shock (bonds stop hedging equities).

### Stagflation overlay

Purpose: defend purchasing power under supply shocks and sticky inflation (Scenario C, sometimes E).

Components:
- Cash/bills (liquidity; avoid long nominal duration)  
- Inflation-linked bonds  
- Gold  
- Broad commodities (measured, diversified)  
- Equity tilt: value/real-asset / pricing power  
- Infrastructure/utilities equities  
- Short‑duration IG credit (avoid long spread duration)

When to activate:
- S2 and S3 both at 2 (sticky inflation + implemented tariffs/retaliation) citeturn1search3turn1search14  
- S4 rising (term premium up / curve steepening)  
- Real yields rising while growth indicators soften (policy trade-off)

When to reduce/exit:
- Inflation expectations re-anchor; S2 drops to 0–1  
- Tariff/fragmentation impulse fades; S3 drops

Key failure mode:
- Demand shock overwhelms supply shock (commodities and value underperform; duration rallies).

### USD-weakness / rotation overlay

Purpose: participate in multi-year repricing away from U.S. assets and reduce USD concentration risk (Scenario D).

Components:
- Broader ex‑US equities (developed + selective EM)  
- Partial unhedged FX exposure (selective; avoid “all-in” FX bets)  
- Non‑USD high-grade sovereigns (short/intermediate)  
- Gold  
- Inflation-linked bonds (if USD weakness is inflationary)  
- Cash/bills (as rebalancing buffer)  
- Select real-assets equity tilt (commodity producers/infrastructure as a style bucket)

When to activate:
- S6 = 2 and sustained; S7 ≥1 and rising citeturn6search0turn6search6  
- U.S. equities underperform globally in common-currency terms for multiple months  
- U.S. term premium rises without corresponding growth acceleration (valuation repricing)

When to reduce/exit:
- Acute stress phase with USD squeeze (S6 drops to 0) and funding stress rises citeturn6search14  
- Rotation evidence fades: S7 back to 0

Key failure mode:
- “Dollar down” thesis is front‑run, then reversed by global recession risk (USD spikes; EM sells off).

### Debt-cycle / term-premium overlay

Purpose: protect when long bonds stop hedging equities because term premium/fiscal dominance becomes the dominant risk (Scenario E, often overlaps C).

Components:
- Cash/bills and short‑duration high grade  
- Inflation-linked bonds  
- Gold  
- Quality/min‑vol equities (cashflow resilience)  
- Infrastructure/utilities (pricing power)  
- Moderate broad commodities exposure (scenario-dependent)  
- Tactical VIX‑futures vol sleeve for correlation spikes  

When to activate:
- S4 = 2 (persistent steepening/auction weakness) citeturn2search2  
- Bond-equity correlation turns positive for a sustained window  
- Inflation expectations stop falling despite weakening growth (policy trap)  
- Policy rhetoric threatens central-bank independence (fiscal dominance risk) citeturn2search17  

When to reduce/exit:
- Term premium stabilizes; auctions improve; S4 returns to 0–1  
- Inflation expectations re-anchor and bonds re-hedge equity

Key failure mode:
- A true deflationary bust arrives (you’re underweight the best-performing asset: long duration).

## Product toolkit

### Cash/bills / short-duration high grade

Portfolio role: liquidity buffer, ballast, “rebalancing fuel”  
Best scenarios: A (as optionality), B, C, E  
Main failure mode: big opportunity cost in strong risk-on rallies  
Practical note: use money-market / short-duration high-grade wrappers; treat it as a strategic tool, not “dead money.”

### High-grade sovereigns

Portfolio role: ballast; crisis convexity (when the hedge works)  
Best scenarios: A (selectively), B (core), D (case-dependent), E (only short/intermediate)  
Main failure mode: long duration fails under stagflation/term-premium shocks (C/E)  
Practical note: split conceptually into **short/intermediate** (funding + low drawdown) versus **long duration** (crisis hedge). “Safe-haven” is joint distribution of **credit quality + market liquidity + FX behavior**; Switzerland/Singapore/Norway are useful case studies, not guarantees.

### Inflation-linked bonds

Portfolio role: inflation hedge with real-rate sensitivity; diversifier in supply shocks  
Best scenarios: C, E; sometimes D (if USD weakness is inflationary)  
Main failure mode: can draw down if real yields rise sharply (policy tightens further)  
Practical note: prefer diversified inflation-linked exposure; be aware you’re still taking duration via real rates.

### Gold

Portfolio role: monetary metal hedge (fiscal/geopolitical tail), portfolio “trust asset”  
Best scenarios: C, D, E; also B (policy tail hedge)  
Main failure mode: can lag in strong disinflation + rising real yields; can chop violently  
Practical note: distinguish gold (monetary) from industrial metals (cycle). Central-bank demand has been unusually strong and persistent in recent years. citeturn3search0turn3search13

### Industrial metals

Portfolio role: cyclical / capex proxy (not a monetary hedge)  
Best scenarios: A (productivity boom + capex), selective C (if supply tight)  
Main failure mode: demand shock (B) hits hardest  
Practical note: treat as growth beta and supply‑constraint expression, not as “currency insurance.”

### Broad commodities

Portfolio role: supply-shock hedge; diversification when inflation is cost-push  
Best scenarios: C; sometimes D/E  
Main failure mode: deflationary recessions (B) and sharp USD spikes  
Practical note: keep broad and sized modestly; this is a regime hedge, not core ballast.

### VIX-futures-linked volatility products

Portfolio role: crisis convexity **only when timed**; hedge against equity drawdowns and vol spikes  
Best scenarios: B, E (and short sharp C shocks)  
Main failure mode: structural decay (“roll/bleed”) in normal markets; wrong-time holding  
Practical note: these products generally implement rolling exposure to VIX futures, not spot VIX; long VIX futures exposure has historically exhibited substantial negative average returns (carry/roll + risk premia), so treat as a trigger-based overlay, not buy-and-hold. citeturn5search0turn5search3  
*Footnote warning:* Avoid levered/inverse volatility ETPs as core tools; path dependence can be lethal.

### Equity styles

Portfolio role: adapt equity beta to regime (return engine + drawdown control)  
Best scenarios: varies by bucket  
Main failure mode: style crowding and regime flips  
Practical note: think in sleeves:
- Quality/min‑vol: ballast-style equity; best in B/E; fine in A  
- Value/real-asset tilt: inflation resilience; best in C/E  
- Ex‑US diversification: key for D; also helps reduce single-country policy risk  
- Defense/cyber: geopolitical + industrial policy exposure; best in C/D  
- Infrastructure/utilities: pricing-power + regulated cashflows; best in C/E

### Credit: investment grade vs high yield

Portfolio role: income + carry (when benign); can be hidden equity beta in stress  
Best scenarios: IG in A; tactically in D; avoid heavy credit beta in B/E  
Main failure mode: demand shock and refinancing stress (B/E) → spreads + defaults; liquidity gaps in “semi-liquid” credit vehicles citeturn2search3  
Practical note: treat HY as **risk-on**, not defensive; upgrade quality when S5 turns.

### Crypto

Portfolio role: optional diversifier; regime-dependent (liquidity beta vs “alt monetary” narrative)  
Best scenarios: A (risk-on liquidity), D/E (only if “fiat credibility” narrative dominates—UNCERTAIN)  
Main failure mode: liquidity tightening and risk-off (B/E) can crush it; narratives can fail abruptly  
Practical note: keep sizing humble; validation would be “holds up during USD weakness and policy stress,” invalidation would be “sells off like high beta every time funding tightens.”

## Failure modes and how this goes wrong

- USD **often spikes first** in acute stress even if the multi-year path is USD weakness. citeturn6search14  
- Long-duration sovereigns can **fail as hedges** in stagflation/term-premium regimes (bonds and equities down together).
